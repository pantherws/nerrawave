# About Nerrawave

**Nerrawave is an AI transcription and audio analysis platform that converts recorded conversations into accurate, editable transcripts and structured insights for researchers, businesses and professionals.**

It was built around a simple idea: recording a conversation should be the beginning of understanding it, not the beginning of hours of additional work.

A recording contains words, but it also contains context. Questions, answers, opinions, experiences, observations and details that may only become significant when considered alongside the rest of the conversation.

Traditional transcription makes those words readable. Modern artificial intelligence makes it possible to do considerably more with them.

Nerrawave brings AI transcription, speaker identification, transcript editing, automated analysis and conversational AI together in a single system.

Upload a recording. Transcribe it. Explore what was said.

The objective is straightforward: make the information contained in recorded conversations easier to access, understand and use.

## What Nerrawave does

### AI audio and video transcription

Nerrawave uses advanced AI speech recognition technology to convert spoken language into written text.

It processes supported audio and video recordings, producing transcripts that can be read, reviewed, edited and used for further analysis.

The system is designed for recorded interviews, focus groups, research sessions, discussions, meetings and other forms of spoken communication.

Instead of manually transcribing recordings or relying on a combination of separate tools, users can move directly from an uploaded recording to a working transcript within the same platform.

### Speaker identification and timestamps

A transcript needs to communicate more than the words that were spoken.

Nerrawave uses speaker diarization to distinguish between participants in a recording, assigning speaker labels to the relevant sections of the conversation.

Timestamps connect the text to its corresponding position in the source recording.

This makes it possible to identify who said something, when it was said and where to find the original passage.

For researchers working with interviews and focus groups, that connection is particularly important. The meaning of a statement often depends on the question that prompted it, the person who said it and the surrounding discussion.

### A transcript editor connected to the recording

Automated transcription is capable, but it is not infallible.

Accents, overlapping speech, unfamiliar names, technical vocabulary, recording quality and background noise can affect recognition accuracy.

Nerrawave includes a transcript editor designed to keep the written text connected to the original audio.

Users can play recordings, navigate through timestamps, review passages, correct transcription errors and adjust speaker labels without having to move between unrelated applications.

The purpose is to make transcript verification a natural part of the workflow rather than a separate administrative task.

### AI transcription analysis

Converting speech into text is only the first stage.

Nerrawave uses AI to examine the content of transcripts and organise the information contained within them.

Depending on the analysis selected, the system can help identify:

* Main themes and recurring topics
* Important statements and observations
* Participant opinions and perspectives
* Questions and responses
* Key quotations
* Patterns within a conversation
* Potential areas of agreement or disagreement
* Sentiment and expressed attitudes
* Summaries and key findings
* Information relevant to specific research questions

These capabilities are particularly useful when the objective is not simply to document a conversation, but to understand what can be learned from it.

AI analysis is intended to assist human interpretation, not replace it. The relevance, meaning and significance of a finding remain matters for the researcher or professional responsible for the work.

### Ask AI

Sometimes the most useful question is one that was not anticipated when a recording was made.

Nerrawave's Ask AI functionality allows users to explore the content of a transcript through natural language questions.

Rather than reading an entire transcript to locate one particular answer, users can ask questions about the recorded discussion.

For example:

* What were the main concerns raised by participants?
* Which product features received the most criticism?
* What did the respondent say about pricing?
* Which reasons were given for switching providers?
* What differences emerged between the participants?
* Which statements support a particular finding?
* What questions remained unanswered?

Ask AI is designed to help users return to the relevant material, explore the information more efficiently and examine possible connections within a conversation.

Where supported, responses can be connected to the underlying transcript and recording so that users can verify the evidence rather than relying exclusively on an AI interpretation.

### Summaries, themes and research findings

A lengthy interview can contain several important ideas dispersed throughout the discussion.

Nerrawave can help organise these into a more accessible structure.

Summaries provide an overview of the conversation. Theme identification helps group related observations. Key quotations provide direct examples of what participants said.

For qualitative researchers, these functions can reduce the time spent locating information before analysis begins.

The objective is not to turn every conversation into a collection of generic bullet points.

It is to make the original material easier to examine and the relevant findings easier to locate.

## From recording to understanding

There is a difference between having a recording, having a transcript and understanding what the recording contains.

A recording preserves the conversation.

A transcript makes the words accessible.

Analysis helps organise those words into information that can be examined, questioned and interpreted.

These are connected tasks, but they have traditionally involved separate stages of work.

A researcher might conduct an interview, send the audio for transcription, receive a written document, correct the transcript, read it, highlight important passages, identify themes and begin developing findings.

Nerrawave is designed to bring more of that process into one environment.

The recording remains the source. The transcript provides access to it. AI helps organise and interrogate its contents.

Human judgement determines what the information means.

## How Nerrawave works

### From audio to insight in one workflow

Nerrawave is designed to handle the process of turning recorded speech into usable information, from the initial upload through transcription, verification and analysis.

### 1. Upload your recording

Upload a supported audio or video file directly through Nerrawave.

The system prepares the recording for speech recognition and subsequent processing.

You do not need to manually type the conversation, divide it into separate passages or prepare a transcript before beginning.

### 2. Generate the transcript

Nerrawave processes the recording using AI speech recognition.

The system identifies spoken content, generates the transcript, distinguishes speakers where possible and associates the text with timestamps.

Processing time depends on recording length, audio quality, complexity and system demand.

The result is a structured transcript connected to its source recording.

### 3. Listen, review and edit

Open the transcript alongside the audio player.

Navigate between sections of the recording, review what was said and make corrections where necessary.

Speaker labels and timestamps help maintain the relationship between the written transcript and the original conversation.

This is especially useful when verifying quotations, unfamiliar terminology, multiple speakers or passages where the audio is unclear.

### 4. Analyse the conversation

Once a transcript is available, Nerrawave's AI analysis tools can help organise the recorded material.

Generate summaries, identify subjects discussed, examine recurring themes, locate meaningful quotations and explore participant statements.

The analysis provides a starting point for deeper examination rather than claiming to produce definitive research conclusions.

### 5. Ask questions about the recording

Use Ask AI to explore the transcript through questions.

Ask about a particular subject, compare statements within the discussion or locate passages relevant to your work.

Instead of manually searching through every line, the system helps you find the information you are looking for.

Important responses should be checked against the transcript and original audio, particularly where they will support published findings, client recommendations or consequential decisions.

### 6. Review and use the results

Return to the original recording whenever clarification is needed.

Review the transcript, refine your interpretation and use the resulting material in your research, reports, notes or other work.

Supported download and export options make the information available beyond the transcription interface.

The entire process is designed to be accessible through a browser, without requiring a separate transcription application or complex technical setup.

## What makes Nerrawave different

### 1. Built around recordings, not meetings

A significant part of the AI transcription market is focused on meeting assistants.

Platforms such as Otter.ai and Fireflies.ai provide transcription, summaries and other features within broader meeting and workplace productivity workflows.

Nerrawave takes a more focused approach.

Its starting point is the recorded conversation itself, whether that recording comes from a research interview, focus group, customer discussion or another source.

The emphasis is on what happens after a conversation has been recorded, particularly when the recording needs to be reviewed and analysed.

Nerrawave is not intended to become another calendar assistant or meeting management platform.

### 2. Transcription and analysis belong together

A transcript is useful, but it is often not the final objective.

For a researcher, the transcript is part of the material from which findings will be developed.

Nerrawave combines transcription, review and AI analysis so that users can move between the original recording, the written conversation and its possible meaning without repeatedly exporting information into separate tools.

This creates a more continuous workflow between capturing what was said and examining why it matters.

### 3. The recording remains the evidence

AI can summarise a conversation, but summaries are interpretations.

They can omit details, misrepresent emphasis or overlook an important exception.

Nerrawave is designed around keeping the source material accessible through timestamps, speaker labels and transcript navigation.

This allows users to examine the statements behind a summary or finding.

For qualitative research, where quotations and contextual interpretation matter, maintaining a clear relationship between the analysis and the original conversation is fundamental.

### 4. The researcher remains in control

Nerrawave uses AI to reduce repetitive work, not to substitute automated conclusions for professional judgement.

The transcript can be reviewed and corrected. The source recording can be revisited. AI generated summaries and themes can be evaluated against the underlying material.

The system helps identify and organise information.

It does not determine whether a research finding is valid, whether a particular interpretation is justified or what recommendation a client should receive.

Those decisions belong to the people conducting the research.

### 5. Pay for the audio you process

Nerrawave uses a pay as you go pricing model rather than requiring a recurring subscription for standard use.

The intended standard rate is **US$0.10 per audio minute**, with transcription, AI analysis and Ask AI included in the usage offering.

A 60 minute recording therefore has a transcription usage cost of US$6 at that rate.

This approach is particularly relevant to independent researchers, small agencies and businesses whose recording volumes can vary from one project to another.

Users can purchase the processing they need without having to maintain a subscription during periods when they have no recordings to process.

### 6. A focused system rather than an entire research platform

Nerrawave is not designed to replace every part of a research agency's operation.

Research design, recruitment, moderation, client management, reporting, methodology and final interpretation involve specialised work that extends beyond transcription.

Platforms such as Dovetail address broader research analysis and research repository workflows, while services such as Trint address transcription within more extensive media production environments.

Nerrawave concentrates on a narrower part of the process: turning recordings into reliable, accessible transcripts and useful material for analysis.

The goal is to do that job well without requiring users to change the rest of the way they work.

## Why Nerrawave exists

Recording technology has made it remarkably easy to preserve conversations.

A phone can record an interview. A computer can capture a discussion. Video conferencing software can save meetings. Professional recording equipment can preserve hours of detailed research.

The ability to record information is no longer the principal challenge.

The challenge is what happens afterwards.

A recording can be an excellent source of information and an inconvenient way to access it.

To find a particular statement, someone may have to listen through a lengthy file. To compare responses, they may need to examine several transcripts. To identify recurring themes, they may have to review hours of conversation and organise the findings manually.

These tasks are necessary, but much of the work surrounding them is repetitive.

Speech recognition has reduced the burden of transcription.

Modern AI introduces another possibility: software that can help examine the content of a recording, identify relevant information and respond to questions about what was said.

Nerrawave was built around bringing those capabilities together.

Not simply to create transcripts faster, but to make recordings substantially more useful after they have been created.

## The importance of listening

A transcript is not a complete substitute for a recording.

Spoken communication includes pauses, emphasis, uncertainty, interruption, tone and the surrounding context of a conversation.

A statement that appears unambiguous in writing may sound very different when heard.

This is particularly important in qualitative research, where meaning is often found in how a participant explains an experience rather than only in the words used to describe it.

Nerrawave therefore treats the original recording as an important part of the working material.

AI makes the conversation searchable and easier to analyse.

The audio remains available for verification and interpretation.

The objective is not to remove listening from research.

It is to make listening more purposeful.

## From transcription to qualitative research analysis

Qualitative research is concerned with questions that cannot always be reduced to numbers.

Why do customers prefer one product over another?

How do people describe a particular experience?

What causes someone to trust or distrust a service?

Which problems recur across interviews?

How do participants understand a concept, and where do their interpretations differ?

These questions often require researchers to examine individual experiences in depth.

Recorded interviews and focus groups provide that material, but the information is not automatically organised when a recording ends.

A transcript makes the content readable.

AI analysis can then help identify potential themes, summarise relevant passages, locate quotations and assist researchers in comparing what participants have said.

However, frequency is not necessarily importance, and a repeated phrase is not automatically a meaningful finding.

A participant's tone cannot always be reliably classified by software. An apparent pattern may require additional contextual investigation.

Nerrawave is designed to support that process of inquiry while leaving the interpretation in human hands.

The purpose of AI is to help researchers spend less time locating information and more time understanding it.

## Why accurate transcription still matters

The quality of analysis depends partly on the quality of the information being analysed.

An incorrectly transcribed word can change the meaning of a sentence.

An incorrectly attributed speaker can alter the interpretation of a discussion.

A missing negative can reverse the meaning of a statement entirely.

AI transcription should therefore be treated as a working representation of the recording rather than an automatically perfect record.

Nerrawave provides editing and playback tools to help users verify the transcript against the original audio.

For routine recordings, relatively little intervention may be necessary.

For specialised terminology, complex discussions, overlapping speakers or important research quotations, closer review may be appropriate.

The system is designed to make that review manageable.

Sound travels through waves.

A recording captures those waves and preserves information that can be played back long after the original conversation has ended.

The wave is both a literal reference to audio and a broader symbol of movement, transmission and change.

Together, Nerra and Wave form a name for a technology built around sound and what can be discovered within it.

## The past and the future of sound

There is a reason Nerrawave's visual identity draws from analogue audio equipment, cassette tapes, vintage radios and recording technology.

For much of the history of recorded sound, the objective was preservation.

A recording allowed a voice, performance or conversation to be captured and replayed.

Magnetic tape gave people a physical way to store sound. The cassette made recorded audio portable and personal. Digital recording made capturing, copying and distributing audio considerably easier.

Each development changed how sound could be stored and accessed.

Artificial intelligence represents another change.

A recording can now become text. The text can be searched. Speakers can be distinguished. Conversations can be summarised. Questions can be asked about their contents.

The recording is no longer limited to playback.

Nerrawave's retro futurist design reflects that relationship between older recording technology and modern computational intelligence.

The analogue references represent where recording came from.

The technology represents what recording can become.

The interface is deliberately contemporary, with the visual character of audio equipment rather than unnecessary complexity.

The objective is a product that feels distinctive without making the work more difficult.

**Every sound has a story.**

Nerrawave is built to help people find the information within it.
